         RESTAURANT CHAIN ANALYSIS — TASK EXPLANATION & METHODOLOGY

PROJECT TITLE:   Restaurant Chain Identification, Rating & Popularity Analysis
DATASET:         Global Restaurant Dataset (9,551 records, 21 features)
DELIVERABLE:     Exploratory data analysis with visualizations & summary report

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1 — TASK DESCRIPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The objective of this project is divided into two core analytical tasks:

  TASK A — Chain Identification
  ─────────────────────────────
  Determine whether restaurant chains are present in the dataset. A restaurant
  chain is defined as any brand or business name that appears across two or more
  distinct outlet records — i.e., restaurants with identical names operating at
  multiple locations.

  TASK B — Rating & Popularity Analysis
  ──────────────────────────────────────
  Once chains are identified, analyze and compare them across multiple business-
  relevant dimensions: aggregate rating, customer votes (as a proxy for
  popularity), geographic spread (cities and countries covered), and price
  positioning. Benchmark chain performance against standalone restaurants.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2 — DATASET DESCRIPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dataset contains records of restaurants from multiple countries, with each
row representing a single restaurant outlet. Key fields include:

  - Restaurant ID        : Unique identifier per outlet
  - Restaurant Name      : Brand or establishment name
  - Country Code         : Numeric country identifier
  - City                 : Operating city
  - Cuisines             : Type(s) of food served
  - Aggregate Rating     : Score on a 0.0–4.9 scale
  - Rating Text          : Categorical label (Excellent, Very Good, Good,
                           Average, Poor, Not Rated)
  - Votes                : Number of customer ratings/reviews received
  - Average Cost for two : Estimated cost for a two-person dining experience
  - Price Range          : Ordinal price band (1 = lowest, 4 = highest)
  - Has Table Booking    : Whether advance table booking is available
  - Has Online Delivery  : Whether online delivery is offered

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3 — METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1: Data Loading & Quality Assessment
─────────────────────────────────────────
The dataset was loaded using the pandas library. An initial quality check was
performed to assess:
  - Shape and dimensionality
  - Presence and count of null/missing values
  - Data types and ranges for each column
  - Distribution of the target variables (rating, votes)

STEP 2: Chain Identification
─────────────────────────────
Each restaurant name was tallied across the dataset using value_counts(). Any
name appearing in two or more records was classified as a chain. The dataset was
then split into two subsets:
  - Chain outlets    : Records belonging to a multi-location brand
  - Standalone restaurants : Unique brands with only one record

STEP 3: Chain Aggregation
──────────────────────────
For each identified chain, the following metrics were computed by grouping on
restaurant name:
  - Total outlet count (how many records/locations exist)
  - Mean aggregate rating across all outlets
  - Total and average votes
  - Number of distinct cities and countries covered
  - Average cost for two

This produced a comprehensive chain-level summary table (chain_summary.csv).

STEP 4: Comparative Rating Analysis
─────────────────────────────────────
Ratings for chain outlets and standalone restaurants were compared using:
  - Histograms with density curves overlaid
  - Box plots showing median, IQR, and outlier spread
  - Descriptive statistics (mean, median, standard deviation)

Ratings of 0.0 were excluded from statistical comparisons as they represent
establishments with no ratings recorded ("Not Rated" category).

STEP 5: Popularity Analysis (Votes)
─────────────────────────────────────
Customer votes were used as a proxy for popularity and engagement. Two views
were taken:
  - Total votes per chain (raw reach)
  - Average votes per outlet (engagement normalized by scale)

This distinction reveals whether a chain's popularity is driven by scale or
by the strength of individual locations.

STEP 6: Geographic Analysis
─────────────────────────────
The number of distinct cities each chain operates in was extracted to understand
geographic penetration. This was compared against total outlet count to
distinguish between dense city-cluster strategies and wider geographic spread.

STEP 7: Rating Category Breakdown
───────────────────────────────────
For the top 10 chains by outlet count, the proportion of outlets falling into
each rating category (Excellent, Very Good, Good, Average, Poor, Not Rated) was
computed and visualized as a stacked percentage bar chart. This reveals the
quality consistency profile of each chain.

STEP 8: Multi-Metric Heatmap
──────────────────────────────
To enable holistic comparison across all key metrics simultaneously, a normalized
heatmap was produced for the top 15 chains. Each metric was min-max normalized
to a 0–1 scale, with the actual value annotated within each cell. This provides
an at-a-glance competitive landscape view.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4 — KEY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Chain Presence
     734 distinct restaurant chains were identified in the dataset, accounting
     for 2,839 outlet records — approximately 29.7% of the full dataset. The
     remaining 6,712 records represent standalone, independent restaurants.

  2. Scale of Operations
     The largest chain holds 83 outlet records, followed by chains with 79 and
     63 locations respectively. A long-tail distribution is observed — the
     majority of chains operate with fewer than 10 outlets.

  3. Rating Comparison
     Standalone restaurants carry a marginally higher mean aggregate rating
     (3.49) compared to chain restaurants (3.34). This suggests that chains,
     while benefiting from brand recognition and scale, may face quality
     consistency challenges across locations.

  4. Highest-Rated Chains
     Among chains with at least 5 outlets, the top performers achieve average
     ratings of 4.34 to 4.58, placing them firmly in the "Excellent" category.
     This demonstrates that scale and quality are not mutually exclusive.

  5. Votes vs Rating Divergence
     Chains with the highest total votes do not consistently rank highest in
     ratings. The most-voted chains attract large customer engagement, but
     quality perception as reflected in ratings can differ. This highlights
     two separate competitive axes: brand reach and service quality.

  6. Rating Category Distribution
     Across the top 10 chains, the dominant rating category at the outlet level
     is "Average", with a smaller proportion of outlets achieving "Excellent"
     or "Very Good" ratings. This points to a systemic challenge in maintaining
     premium quality standards at scale.

  7. Geographic Penetration
     Leading chains operate across multiple cities, with some covering 10 or
     more distinct cities within the dataset. However, high outlet counts do
     not always correspond to wide city coverage — some chains are heavily
     concentrated within a small number of cities.
